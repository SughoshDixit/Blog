It's long overdue. I wanted write about that person, who shaped me who I am today, the person who had to face such difficult life situations, abnormalities, volatilities, gave a son to the country, was the smartest human being I know, handled four children single-handedly. Introducing Vanamala Dixit, my very own Ajji!

Born in 1945, she was the second daughter to big Deshpande family, in the district of Bijapur (Vijayapura). She was a brilliant student in her school and she aimed to pass metric (10th standard) with flying colours at that time period. At that time period having a girl child of age was considered a burden across the society as the girl child would eventually get married and go to her husband's house. Similarly although my ajji did not want to marry she was compulsated to get married at the mere age of 15, around the time she was 8th grade, not giving a second thought about the credibility, capability of the groom, not giving her enough time to complete her dream of completing mere metric (10th standard) which at that time was a gold standard. 

Eventually she got married to my Ajja in 1959, and she had her first child the following year (a son), further she had 3 more children while a 5th was miscarried due to an accident. I being the child of the 4th son, I have first hand spent my entire life until my 23 years with her, under her tutilage of how to lead a life, what to become in life, values and principles in life.

She always kept on emphasizing about one thing - "become whatever in life, but become a good human being first". That has remained in me today, and you can assume I'm the exact reflection of her. She was a strong woman, in a patriarchical society it was her who lead the house and was the dominant figure, and she had earned the right to do that. I can proudly say it was her who lifted us from poverty to whoever we are today. 

In her early marriage days she concieved 4 kids almost continuously - which now is unimaginable. At the age 25 she already had 4 kids and a 5th miscarried. She run her house in the town of Harihara almost individually - at that time my grandfather her husband (was ver irresponsible). Thank god, he did not have any bad habits - but he was a proper movie geek, almost going to theatres 5-6 times a week. To the level where both of them used to fight over being repsonsible now that they had not 1 not 2 but 4 kids. Life was really hard financially where affording new clothes for a festival was difficult and my grandfather had to borrow it from someone. Life was hard in post independence era in India.

Once all her kids came of age and with tremendous hardships she had managed to raise 4 kids by giving whatever education she could give them - her second kid Ajit managed to join the armed forces - specifically the BSF. My father her last child managed to crack into the Indian Army.
